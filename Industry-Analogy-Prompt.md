# Turn a Topic into an Industry Analogy

Copy the prompt below into an AI chat and replace `[ENTER TOPIC]`. A topic can be an everyday activity, an observation, a story, or an industry concept. The other fields are optional.

---

## Your input

**Topic:** [ENTER TOPIC]
**Industry to explain (optional):**
**Audience (optional):**

## Your task

Use this topic to help someone understand an industry through a clear, insightful analogy. Go beyond surface similarities: identify the underlying patterns that explain why things happen and how people can make better decisions.

If I supply only a topic, proceed without asking questions:
- If the topic is an everyday activity or observation, choose one industry with a strong, useful connection. Default to marketing or business only when the connection fits naturally.
- If the topic is an industry or professional concept, choose a familiar everyday situation that makes it easier to understand.
- If I name both, use that pairing.
- Assume the reader is an intelligent beginner. Make your chosen pairing clear in the opening sentence.

## How to build the explanation

1. Identify the mechanisms in the source topic: what attracts attention, influences behavior, creates constraints, builds trust, reduces risk, or determines success. Consider resources, location, timing, incentives, feedback, competition, cooperation, and adaptation where relevant. Do not force every category into the answer.
2. Match those mechanisms to specific industry concepts. For each connection, establish what corresponds to what, why the relationship holds, and what practical decision it helps explain. Similar vocabulary alone is not a meaningful connection.
3. Develop three to five strong connections around one coherent analogy. Prefer a few well-explained connections over a long list of loose comparisons.
4. Add at least two useful concepts I did not explicitly mention. Integrate them naturally and explain their relevance rather than merely naming them.
5. Include a concrete example and at least one practical action the reader can take. Distinguish visible activity from meaningful results when that distinction fits.
6. Check where the analogy breaks down. Do not treat an analogy as proof or assume that people behave exactly like animals, machines, or other elements of the source topic. Briefly mention a boundary if omitting it would mislead the reader.
7. Preserve my useful observations without repeating questionable claims as facts. Qualify or correct oversimplifications gently. Verify uncertain or specialized factual claims when research tools are available; otherwise avoid making the explanation depend on them. Never invent evidence or statistics.

## Output

Write exactly **two polished paragraphs**, approximately **220–320 words total**, unless I request another length or format.

- In the first paragraph, introduce the analogy and explain its strongest connections to the industry.
- In the second paragraph, deepen the explanation with the additional concepts, practical implications, and a meaningful measure of success where appropriate.
- Use clear, conversational language, concrete details, and smooth transitions.
- Explain necessary industry terms in context.
- Return only the finished prose: no headings, bullet points, planning notes, or commentary about your process.
- Avoid forced comparisons, generic motivational advice, and claims that a single tactic guarantees success.

## Example inputs

- `Fishing in a pond` — choose a suitable industry and explain it through the behavior and decisions involved in fishing.
- `Fishing in a pond | Industry: marketing | Audience: small business owners` — connect fish location, food sources, and bait selection to specific marketing decisions, then extend the analogy with relevant concepts such as intent, trust, timing, conversion, or retention.
- `Cybersecurity` — choose a familiar everyday analogy that explains core cybersecurity decisions without implying that the analogy captures every technical detail.

Apply these instructions to **my topic**, not to the examples unless I choose one.
